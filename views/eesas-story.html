Eesa's Story

The Heart and Legacy Behind the Childhood Cancer Care Foundation

"Eesa was deeply loved." That is the very first thing Eesa’s mother says about him.

From the moment he was born, he filled his parents’ lives with joy and a profound sense of completeness. He was raised with care, intention, and presence. As the first child and grandchild, he owned every room he entered. From his first words to his first steps, every milestone was celebrated with immense joy. He had everyone wrapped around his little fingers. Like all parents, his mother and father had many dreams and plans for his future.

Eesa filled his family's lives with a profound sense of joy and completeness.

An Unexpected Turn

Eesa was just two months shy of his second birthday when his life took an unexpected turn.

One morning, his mother noticed that his diaper was stained red with bloody urine. What followed moved incredibly quickly. An ultrasound was done the same day, followed by a CT scan, and then a biopsy. Eesa was diagnosed with Stage 2 Neuroblastoma.

The best doctors in Pakistan were consulted, and his parents were assured that the latest world-class treatment was available in his home city. Treatment started right away.

Hospitals rapidly became familiar spaces. Medical words became a permanent part of his daily language—PICC line, IV, cannula, chemotherapy. These are words no child should ever have to learn, yet Eesa carried them with quiet acceptance and a grace rarely seen in someone so young.

"In the face of circumstances no child should ever face, Eesa showed a gentle patience that surprised everyone around him."

Eesa underwent multiple cycles of chemotherapy, which resulted in long hospital stays as his body struggled to recover. His blood counts would fall, leaving him connected to IV lines for days for hydration and medication. For a child his age, being confined to a hospital bed was unimaginably difficult, yet he adapted in ways that continuously surprised those around him.

His parents learned how to make a home for him inside hospital rooms. They learned how to distract him, comfort him, and hold steady when he could not understand why his days were filled with needles and machines instead of visits to the park.

Eesa carried his difficult journey with a quiet acceptance and grace.

The Battle Deepens

Surgery followed, and recovery was slower and harder than expected. Still, there was hope. The doctors remained optimistic. Little Eesa was a fighter. He was expected to go on to live a cancer-free life, and for a time, it truly seemed possible that the worst was behind them. He began to recover, and life slowly started to resemble something normal again.

But six months into remission, a routine follow-up scan revealed the devastating news: the cancer had returned.

This time, the disease was far more aggressive. The treatment intensified. Eesa traveled abroad for care, accompanied by parents who carried hope and fear side by side. Months of chemotherapy followed in the hope that he would become eligible for a bone marrow transplant. Despite every single effort, his body did not respond as hoped, and the transplant could not go ahead.

Eventually, chemotherapy was stopped.

Until then, Eesa had shown few signs of the disease itself; much of his suffering had come from the treatments meant to save him. But in the weeks that followed, the cancer progressed rapidly. His little body started giving up. A bump appeared on his head. Pain began in his legs, and he slowly lost the ability to walk. Radiation therapy was given, but the cancer continued to spread, reaching the bones of his skull—changing his appearance and causing visible swelling around his eyes. Eesa became completely bedridden.

Even then, he remained gentle and patient.

Surrounded by Love

As his pain increased, his parents stayed close, learning how to ease his discomfort and hold him through the long nights. He was started on oral morphine and required frequent blood transfusions. He longed to be in familiar spaces, close to family. Wishing to travel to his grandparents’ home in Lahore, his parents made that possible for him.

Eesa spent his final days surrounded by absolute love. Those who walked alongside him witnessed a child of remarkable patience. His parents watched him adapt again and again to circumstances no child should ever have to face. They began grieving him long before he was gone, carrying a quiet sorrow while still caring for him fully.

Eesa passed away peacefully at home, in his father’s lap, surrounded by the people who loved him most. He did not fight loudly. He did not resist or rage. He endured quietly. And in that quiet endurance, Eesa showed what true courage looks like when it is held, protected, and loved by those closest to him.

Eesa's story is one of courage and resilience that continues to guide our mission.

The Birth of a Legacy

Sometimes, when life throws an unimaginable tragedy at you, it shakes you to your core and makes rebuilding feel impossible. But Eesa’s story, and the story of his parents, is one of deep courage and resilience. That resilience did not die with Eesa—it was born there.

What Eesa left behind was not only love and grief, but the beginning of a powerful legacy. It is a legacy destined to touch, support, and transform many lives through the Childhood Cancer Care Foundation.
